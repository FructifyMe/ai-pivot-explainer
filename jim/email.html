One genuinely notable shell event this cycle — a well-known financial-media company going public through a dormant shell — plus a regression name taking its next step.

DATZ World Holdings Corp. (LBUY) — RagingBull goes public via the LeafBuyer shell. This is the cleanest reverse-merger stack of the cycle. LeafBuyer Technologies — a long-dormant cannabis-tech shell that had earlier been "AP Event Inc." — completed a merger on June 8 with RagingBull.com, LLC, the well-known financial-education and trading-newsletter business. The June 12 8-K carries the full pivot stack: completion of the acquisition, a 156-for-1 reverse split, a new board and officer slate, and a name change to "DATZ World Holdings." RagingBull's former owners now hold about 95% of the company. This is a real operating business taking over a public shell — a textbook Stage-3 reverse merger. The catch for us is that the stock trades over-the-counter (no live exchange quote), so it isn't cleanly tradable in a normal brokerage app without a phone-in, and OTC shells of this kind are notoriously thin and volatile.

Rubico Inc. (RUBI) — the dead-tape regression name takes its next step. RUBI, the ~$2M Marshall Islands shell we've tracked for weeks, filed a 6-K on June 12 announcing a reverse stock split, and Trillium Trading (the proprietary trading firm that took a disclosed stake the week before) amended its 13G the same day. A reverse split is a classic Stage-3 housekeeping move that usually precedes a name/ticker change. The tape, however, is unconvincing: the +29% pop we flagged on June 12 fully reversed, and the stock is back around $0.50. So the paper trail is progressing while the price is not — exactly the kind of low-conviction setup that belongs on a watch list, not in a portfolio.

ERock, Inc. (EROC) — clean deSPAC, not a lottery. A June 15 charter-stack 8-K surfaced for ERock (formerly "Enchanted Rock"), a real natural-gas microgrid / backup-power company that recently came public. The filing is routine post-listing housekeeping (charter, equity plan, insider grants), the company has an actual operating business, and it trades around $17. This is the "good deSPAC" archetype — a slow operating-company re-rating, not a hit-and-run pivot — so we log it as a reference, the same bucket as FedEx Freight and Octave.
