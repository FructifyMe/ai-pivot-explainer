  • A drug-distribution company turns into "DataMeds AI" this morning — its new ticker MEDS starts trading today. Wellgistics Health, a Nasdaq-listed pharmacy-distribution and health-tech company, filed paperwork Monday changing its name to DataMeds AI, Inc. — and the stock was already up about 29% in pre-market trading before the new symbol even went live. This is the single most-watched species of corporate rebranding this scan tracks: a struggling healthcare logistics business wrapping itself in an AI name. Sometimes these renames mark a real strategy shift; more often the pop fades once the market asks what actually changed. Nothing in the filing describes a new business — it is, so far, a name change only.
  • A second rename goes live today too: Avalon GloboCare is now "Change Agents Corporation," ticker CHGA. This one is a tiny company — about $3 million in total market value, trading at 28 cents — and the filing says nothing about what the new business will be. A rename with no announced business is a watch-and-wait, never a buy.
  • The ghost of Lordstown Motors just came back as a wealth-management firm. Nu Ride Inc. — the shell left over after the Lordstown Motors electric-truck bankruptcy — completed the purchase of a financial-advisory firm and renamed itself Stark Novus Financial. This is a textbook "zombie shell reanimation": an empty bankruptcy shell (kept alive largely for its tax losses) acquiring its way into a completely different industry.
  • Yesterday's two big pump-and-fade warnings. Ohmyhome, a tiny Singapore property platform, rocketed 223% the same day its shareholders authorized increasing the company's share count to a staggering 100 trillion authorized shares — dilution machinery, not a business plan — and it has already given back nearly half the gain in pre-market. Vivakor, a micro-cap oil-logistics firm, jumped 49% while disclosing the second closing of a $15 million loan package carrying a 20% built-in discount — expensive "death spiral"-style financing on a company worth about $9 million. Both pops are fading this morning. Neither is an opportunity; both are the kind of tape this scan exists to flag as radioactive.
  • Housekeeping: the Aterian shell (taken over by operator David Lazar last week) filed nothing new — still waiting for its next act. A tracked micro-cap called Tianci authorized a reverse stock split of up to 1-for-250, another classic pre-makeover step.
