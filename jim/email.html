1 · Today's headline call

Read this first

Read this first

Three companies changed their names this week, and the market shrugged at all three. Lakeside Holding — a small Chicago-area freight forwarder — became "Quanome Technologies" and started trading under QNME on Tuesday. Plus Therapeutics became "Cerenome" (CNSY) the same day. Both are Nasdaq-listed. Neither announced a new business to go with the new name.

QNME is the more interesting one, and the more damning. A cargo-forwarding company adopting a name that sounds like a quantum-computing startup is the single most recognizable move in the rebrand-shell playbook. It executed cleanly — Nevada certificate of amendment, no shareholder vote needed, ticker live the next morning. And the stock is down about 25% in pre-market trade. That's the tell: the rebrand happened, and nobody showed up to buy it. A name change without a business behind it is just a name change.

The one filing with real substance is the one nobody can trade. Invech Holdings — a Nevada shell that has already been Pan Asia Communications, Hubei Pharmaceutical, and Amersin Life Sciences — disclosed on Monday that its sole officer and director sold 88 million shares (75.9% of the company) plus the entire preferred class to a new owner, and filed the formal notice that the whole board is being replaced. That combination is the textbook opening move of a shell takeover. It is also quoted nowhere with a live price, which is precisely why these things are hard to profit from.

Meanwhile the tape is punishing yesterday's winners. Autonomix Medical closed up 434% and is down 26% pre-market. China SXT closed up 60% and is down 30%. Adagio Medical, up 74%, is down 18%. Ten of the fifteen biggest sub-$250M gainers were warrants trading for pennies — mechanical noise, not news. The round-trip is the dominant pattern of the week.

Bottom line: nothing on this scan clears the bar today. Every fresh filing is either untradable, already dumping, or corporate housekeeping dressed up as a catalyst.
