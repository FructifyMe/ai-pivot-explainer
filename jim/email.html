Read this first

  • A $24M cancer biotech tripled by agreeing to stop being a cancer biotech. NextCure (NXTC) jumped +202% Tuesday after signing a reverse merger with private Avere Therapeutics — and the same filing discloses it's cutting a "substantial majority" of its own workforce while the deal closes. Read those two items together and the picture is clean: the science didn't win, the Nasdaq listing did. The private company is buying the ticker; the host organism gets wound down. It's the classic biotech shell hand-off, caught the day it was signed. Fading −24% pre-market to ~$5.
  • The mirror-image deal on the same day. Senti Biosciences (SNTI, ~$0.97) is selling substantially all of its business and pipeline to an affiliate of its largest stockholder — and staying public as a slimmed-down husk holding some IP. Where NextCure's shell is getting a new tenant, Senti's is being emptied out with the landlord already holding 54.6% of the keys. This is the SOBR pattern (deliberate shell formation) executed by insiders instead of a wind-down committee.
  • Speaking of SOBR — the empty shell is getting bid again. SOBR Safe, last week's "we're discontinuing all operations on purpose" special, is +34% pre-market today at $1.57 on 17M shares — with zero new filings. The market is bidding up a vacant Nasdaq listing before any new tenant has shown up. That's how much a clean shell is worth in this tape.
  • The boat-company merger has turned into a squeeze. Twin Vee (VEEE) — Monday's +416% "real merger, boring kind" — added another +55% Tuesday and is +17% pre-market at ~$45. Nothing new was filed except merger paperwork. When a $20M float meets a definitive agreement, arithmetic stops mattering for a few days. Still not a pivot; still just physics.
  • A pivot died in public. Perpetuals.com (PDC), a Tokyo crypto-derivatives micro, terminated its letter of intent to acquire Alt5 Sigma — the would-be transformation deal is dead. The stock rose +26% Tuesday anyway. Sometimes the tape simply doesn't read the filing.
  • Quieter tells: Amaze Holdings (9 cents) set a 1-for-8 reverse split for July 24 — the standard pre-makeover haircut; Genprex filed the same species of split paperwork; Rubico's resale registration went effective (the serial-reverse-split machine rolls on); and Myseum's warrants jumped +30% while the common quietly crept to $2.69 — someone is positioning in the cheapest expression of an April favorite.
