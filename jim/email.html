Read this first

  • The textbook "zombie company costume change" fired in size this week: ClearOne (CLRO), a struggling conference-room audio hardware maker, agreed to a reverse merger with Cortigent — a brain-computer-interface (BCI) neurostimulation spinout of Vivani Medical — and will rebrand as Cortigent Holdings under a new ticker (CRGT). The stock went from ~$3.60 on July 2 to ~$13.84 by Tuesday's close, roughly +170% in three sessions on enormous volume. The catch: legacy holders get diluted to ~13-14% of the combined company, and a law firm is already probing deal fairness. Classic pattern — dying hardware business, hot new story, big pop, big dilution.
  • A second one listed the same day: House of Doge (HODO) — the commercial arm of the Dogecoin Foundation — completed its reverse merger into Brag House Holdings and now trades on Nasdaq. Unlike CLRO it fell ~27% on day two as 64 million new merger shares hit the story. Same playbook, opposite tape.
  • The CLRO pop opened what we call a "copycat window" — after a big pivot pop, other tiny shells tend to file their own rebrands within days. Right on cue: CalEthos renamed itself TerraVolt Holdings (energy-flavored name, OTC shell) and Shorepower Technologies closed its merger to become Aeternum Health (health pivot, OTC). Both are off-exchange and untouchable for now — but they're the fuse-lighting stage of the same script.
  • One quieter tell: Lakeshore Acquisition III (LCCC) filed its definitive merger proxy to become CPRO Electronics (Korean edge-AI security) — the shareholder vote is now scheduled, moving that deal a formal step closer to closing.
  • Bottom line: the pattern is live and paying this week — but the money was made by holding BEFORE the announcement, and the post-pop entries (CLRO at 4x in 3 days) are where people get hurt.
