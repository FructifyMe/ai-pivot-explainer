  • Amazon's cloud arm just put its name on one of our longest-tracked rebrands — and the stock went DOWN. Myseum.AI, a tiny company we've followed since June through its transformation into an AI photo-album platform, announced Wednesday that Amazon Web Services has committed to fund a portion of the development of its privacy-first AI media tool. A named mega-cap backer is exactly the kind of catalyst this scan waits for — but read the fine print: AWS is funding "a portion of development," with no dollar figure disclosed. The market noticed the vagueness too: the stock actually slipped about 4% on the news. When a company gets the best headline of its life and the price falls, that's the tape telling you the market wanted dollars, not press releases.
  • A freshly de-SPACed company called CID HoldCo just executed the full "makeover" playbook in a single day. On Wednesday it filed one 8-K covering a $6 million preferred-stock private placement, three brand-new classes of preferred stock, charter amendments, and officer changes — all at once. Multi-tier preferred structures like this are the same architecture we saw when veteran shell operator David Lazar took control of Aterian earlier this month. Nobody knows yet what gets built inside; the filing stack is the tell that something is being staged.
  • Speaking of Aterian — it crashed 38% Tuesday. The consumer-brands company that Lazar took 95.8% control of last week collapsed on no new filings. This is worth remembering: in our two months of tracking, the "control-operator takes over a shell" pattern has produced zero cleanly tradable winners and several wrecks like this one.
  • Two Chinese micro-caps ran hundreds of percent on classic warning-sign paperwork. Zhongchao, a small education company, nearly tripled the same day it disclosed a share issuance giving its CEO 99.45% of the voting power — it's already down 35% pre-market. Zhibao, an insurance-tech penny stock, jumped 45% after teasing a "potential buyer" it hasn't actually signed a deal with. Both are the pump-around-paperwork species this scan exists to flag, not opportunities.
  • The makeover assembly line keeps humming. Digital Brands Group (a struggling apparel retailer) set a 1-for-40 reverse split for Friday after three 8-Ks in a week; Picard Medical (the artificial-heart maker) scheduled a 1-for-50 reverse split; Greek shipping micro-cap C3is filed yet another reverse split alongside a new stock offering. Reverse splits are the standard pre-makeover haircut — the interesting filing is the one that comes after.
  • Housekeeping: last week's two big renames keep fading — DataMeds AI (ex-Wellgistics) is down to $3.30 from its debut pop, and 28-cent Change Agents Corp still hasn't told anyone what its business will be. The KIDZ AI convertible-note dilution machine registered more shares for resale and hit new lows under 50 cents.
