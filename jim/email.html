  • A biotech shell just merged with an AI company and printed 1.6 BILLION new shares to pay for it. Liminatus Pharma (LIMN, a failing biological-products micro-cap) closed an amended merger with InnocsAI LLC on July 6th — the filing says existing InnocsAI members get "up to" 1.6 billion shares of the combined company, split between common stock and a new non-voting preferred class specifically designed to avoid the vote-counting math that much dilution would normally trigger. The stock popped 29% this morning, then reversed and is down 16% net on the day. This is the same biotech-to-AI listing-swap pattern that's hit four or five names this cycle — the twist here is the dilution is on a different order of magnitude than anything else logged.
  • A shell company quietly renamed itself to a drone company today — nobody will be able to trade it. Hallmark Venture Group, flagged five weeks ago when its board got swapped out in a classic shell handoff, filed papers today changing its name to SDR Drone, Inc. That's the exact playbook step (control change, then rename into a hot sector) this scan exists to catch — except the stock only trades over the counter, meaning ordinary brokerage accounts can't buy it without a phone call to a broker, if at all. The pattern worked; the trade isn't accessible.
  • Jet.AI's four-figure percentage pop this week is still deflating. After spiking 580% Wednesday on a completed asset sale, the stock is down another 16% today. The float-squeeze physics that drove the spike work in both directions once the initial buyers who didn't know why it was moving start selling.
  • A logistics/insurance shell that's been diluting itself with penny-stock financings for months just cratered 48% in a single session — Planet Green Holdings, flagged three days ago for a small capital raise, gave back essentially all the value the raise was supposed to fund. It's an example of the more common outcome in this space: the financing happens, the promised pivot doesn't show up fast enough, and the float gets crushed.
  • A tiny Marshall Islands shipping company has done two reverse stock splits in ten days — Robin Energy is down another 22% today with a fresh split filed July 7th and again July 10th. This is the same "serial reverse-split machine" pattern already logged on a couple of other foreign micro-caps this year: a company keeps shrinking its share count to stay listing-compliant without ever describing what business it's actually pivoting toward.
  • Quieter tells: Glucotrack — last week's "biotech shell absorbed a private company, but nobody noticed" story — ticked up for the first time since the deal closed, a possible early sign the market is starting to price it in. Quantum Cyber, a name that fully played out its pump-and-fade months ago, quietly filed paperwork this week that its drone subsidiary is buying real estate in Connecticut — a sign a "completed" shell story can still generate small follow-on news well after the fact.
