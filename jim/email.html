  • A struggling commodity-trading company just bought an AI predictive-analytics business and took on a $100 million toxic loan to do it — the stock is up 90%+ over two sessions. Sadot Group, an $18-24M food/commodity-trading micro-cap, filed papers Friday saying it bought "TradeIQ" — AI software for predicting commodity trades — from a Hong Kong private company for $6 million, paid partly in stock and partly in a new class of preferred shares. To fund it, the company also signed up for up to $100 million in convertible notes from an unnamed institutional buyer, with terms that let the lender convert into new stock at a discount and reset that discount if the company issues shares even cheaper later — a well-known dilution trap. The stock popped 77% Friday and another 29% today on the news alone, before any of the financing math catches up with it. This is the fifth or sixth "struggling operating company announces AI pivot funded by toxic convertible debt" story this scan has caught this cycle, and none of the earlier ones have ended well for anyone who bought the pop.
  • A shell company changed its home address from Cyprus to Ohio the same day it got a new CEO — a name to watch. Londax Corp, a tiny dormant company previously registered in Cyprus, announced a change-of-control filing naming a new sole director and CEO, a serial small-public-company operator who runs a structured-finance firm and founded a commercial lending company back in 2008. The company's headquarters moved to Cincinnati the same day. This is the exact opening move of the "shell rehabilitation" playbook this scan tracks — an operator with a history of running multiple small public companies takes control of a dormant shell, and a rename into some hot sector follows within weeks to months.

Sow Good Inc., which has been sitting on a leadership change since mid-May with zero follow-up filings, passed its unofficial ten-day decision window today still silent. A drone-tech company called Exyn Technologies passed its own catalyst deadline yesterday with no news — both are now logged as stories that didn't pay off.
  • Quieter tells: a Chinese-owned company that's been publicly renamed three times over several years (most recently into an AI-branded identity) picked up a new activist stakeholder this week — a pattern worth watching, but with zero winners from this specific playbook so far this cycle.
