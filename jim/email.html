  • A once-hot online consumer-brands company just handed itself to a well-known shell operator — its stock is a dollar and its products are gone. Aterian (ticker ATER), the Nasdaq-listed company that once sold Mueller kitchenware, hOmeLabs appliances and the Squatty Potty, completed a two-step deal on Friday: it sold all six of its marquee consumer brands to a buyer called Trademark Global for $18 million cash, and simultaneously issued a special class of preferred stock that handed David Lazar — a serial small-public-company operator — 95.8% ownership and the CEO chair. Existing shareholders now own about 4%. What's left is an empty, clean, still-Nasdaq-tradable shell fully controlled by an operator whose playbook is to rename these shells into a hot sector (crypto, AI, quantum) weeks to months later. This is the single most-watched pattern this scan tracks, and this is the highest-quality version of it in months — the last time one of these Lazar shells was actually listed on a real exchange (a company that became "Quantum Cyber"), it later ran up more than 300%. But the vast majority of these shells go nowhere. There is no new business here yet — only an empty vehicle and an operator with a history. It is a name to watch, not to chase.
