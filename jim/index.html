1. Today's headline call

Read this first

The cybersecurity shell that ran +496% last week just told investors its merger might not close — and sold stock on the way down. Cycurion (CYCU, ~$18M cap) filed two 8-Ks on Friday: one warning that the Halo Privacy / havenX merger may miss its July 31 outside date because the sellers failed to deliver required items, and a second disclosing a $4.5M warrant-inducement financing at $1.35. The stock lost 45% Friday to $0.89. It is bouncing +32% to ~$1.17 pre-market Monday. A deal that "closed" on Tuesday and "may not close" on Friday, plus fresh dilution priced above the current tape, is not a setup — it's a broken one. This is the clearest possible vindication of no-chase discipline: anyone who bought the +496% day is down ~45% in three sessions.

Friday's biggest number on the tape was not a pivot — it was arithmetic. Focus Universal (FCUV) closed +517% at $11.60 and is indicated ~$18.45 Monday pre-market. There is no filing behind it. The company has 702,811 shares outstanding after a reverse split and put out a press release about an "AI Forms Auto-Populate Engine." 93.4 million shares traded against a 703-thousand-share count — the entire company changed hands roughly 133 times in one day. That is a float mechanic, not a business event. The identical pattern (vertical move, no paper) produced T3 Defense's −67% collapse ten days ago; DFNS has now given back nearly everything.

Two reverse splits are worth separating from the noise. Wetour Robotics (WETO) ran +200% Friday on a stack of press releases — a Qualcomm partner-network membership, an NVIDIA Jetson platform announcement, and a warehouse-robotics agreement management says could produce ~$5M of project-level gross profit — and then executed a 1-for-100 share consolidation effective this morning. A PR barrage immediately preceding a 100:1 consolidation is a dilution structure, not a growth story. Separately, Universal Safety Products (UUU, $10.7M cap, NYSE American) filed a 13D/A and shareholder-vote results Friday with shell-rehabber David Lazar named in the paper — a live Lazar shell, and Lazar shells remain the single worst-performing pattern in this scan's history with zero clean winners.

Quietest but most interesting: a $1.3 million company got a new control filing this morning. Valion Bio (VBIO, NASDAQ, $0.31) had a fresh Schedule 13D hit EDGAR on August 3, following a proxy statement and an S-1 registration on July 17. A control stake taken in a Nasdaq-listed shell with a market value barely above one million dollars is the earliest stage of the pattern this scan exists to find — which also means it is far too early to be a trade. Also new: Datz World Holdings (BOTX) filed a charter amendment Friday, six weeks after a full change-of-control stack — the classic prelude to a name and ticker change — though it has no live public quote.

5. How this page works under the hood

This isn't a static page someone manually edits. The flow:

  1. Overnight, an automated job runs each barrel's scan (EDGAR full-text 8-K search, NASDAQ pre-mkt API, federal contract feeds, the SOBR-anchored copycat window).
  2. A Python script (scripts/refresh_jim_briefing.py) takes the morning's outputs, the active briefing markdown, and the current portfolio CSV, and regenerates this HTML using a Jinja-style template.
  3. The script commits the new jim/index.html to this repo and pushes. GitHub Pages rebuilds in ~30 seconds.
  4. The brief is sent as the body of an email to Jim — full content, not a teaser. Arrives between 7:30–8:00 AM ET on trading days. This page is the archive.
  5. Jim replies to the email with ideas / questions. Mike reads every reply and folds them into the next morning's brief. No accounts to create, no portals to log into.

The architecture is intentional. Briefings live as markdown in a private workspace; only the cleaned, public-safe narrative gets published here. No portfolio dollar amounts beyond what's in the document above, no account numbers, no PII.
