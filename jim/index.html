1. Today's headline call

Read this first

A sleepy office-audio company tripled in a day because it's buying a brain-implant maker. ClearOne — a small Utah company that sells conference-room speakerphones — jumped 166% Thursday and kept climbing this morning. The reason: it's acquiring Cortigent, the neurostimulation business descended from Second Sight, the company that once made bionic eyes. Unlike most stories in this corner of the market, there's a real signed merger agreement behind the move. The catch is the move itself: by the time you've heard the story, the stock has already tripled. Paying triple for a company because it announced a deal is how this market takes your money.

This was reverse-merger week. In seventy-two hours, three struggling public companies effectively became different companies: Galera Therapeutics handed its stock-market listing to Obsidian Therapeutics, a cell-therapy firm with real science and fresh institutional money; Ensysce Biosciences absorbed a private biotech overnight; and Sharing Economy International — a shell with no operating business — issued five billion new shares to buy an electric-vehicle design startup. Five. Billion. Shares. The first of those is a real company using a shortcut to go public. The last is a cautionary tale about what "becoming a new company" can mean for the people already holding the stock.

The round-trip parade added two floats. TEN Holdings rose 253% Thursday and was down 38% before this morning's open. Wing Yip Food — a Chinese restaurant-supply company — rose 194% and was down 41% pre-market. Neither had news that explains either direction. Same lesson as YXT last week: when a stock's entire story is its own price chart, you are not early — you are the exit.

And the watchlist name from yesterday did something worth noticing — for the wrong reason. Flash Sports & Media, whose stock jumped on a term sheet to buy a Singapore media company, followed up not with a signed deal but with its chairman doing a live retail-investor interview on a stock-promotion platform. Companies with real deals close them. Companies with moving stock prices promote them. The distinction is the whole game down here.

Bottom line: still no clean buy. The one real transaction (ClearOne) has already tripled, the real biotech (Obsidian) is too big for this scan's mandate, and everything else is dilution machinery or promotion.

5. How this page works under the hood

This isn't a static page someone manually edits. The flow:

  1. Overnight, an automated job runs each barrel's scan (EDGAR full-text 8-K search, NASDAQ pre-mkt API, federal contract feeds, the SOBR-anchored copycat window).
  2. A Python script (scripts/refresh_jim_briefing.py) takes the morning's outputs, the active briefing markdown, and the current portfolio CSV, and regenerates this HTML using a Jinja-style template.
  3. The script commits the new jim/index.html to this repo and pushes. GitHub Pages rebuilds in ~30 seconds.
  4. The brief is sent as the body of an email to Jim — full content, not a teaser. Arrives between 7:30–8:00 AM ET on trading days. This page is the archive.
  5. Jim replies to the email with ideas / questions. Mike reads every reply and folds them into the next morning's brief. No accounts to create, no portals to log into.

The architecture is intentional. Briefings live as markdown in a private workspace; only the cleaned, public-safe narrative gets published here. No portfolio dollar amounts beyond what's in the document above, no account numbers, no PII.
